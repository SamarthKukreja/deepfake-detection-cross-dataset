Cross-Dataset Generalization in Deepfake Detection: A Controlled Study of Spatial, Frequency, and Video-Level Baselines

Samarth Kukreja · Independent Researcher, Faridabad, India

Preprint Code LaTeX Source

Abstract

Deepfake detectors can report strong in-domain scores but still fail when test videos come from a different dataset or are degraded by common image corruptions. This study evaluates that gap with one fixed face-based pipeline and five ImageNet-pretrained RGB backbones: EfficientNet-B4, Xception, ResNet-50, ConvNeXt-Tiny, and ViT-Base.

Experiments use FaceForensics++ (FF++), Celeb-DF (v2), and the DeepFake Detection (DFD) dataset across one source-video-disjoint in-distribution setting and three cross-dataset transfer settings. Metrics are computed at the video level with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals and paired tests on the same held-out videos. Two low-cost probes are also reported: a score-level temporal aggregation baseline and a lightweight frequency-only ResNet-18.

ConvNeXt-Tiny leads in Exp1, Exp2, and Exp4, but its advantage over ResNet-50 is not significant under paired procedures at this single training seed. Source-domain coverage and evaluation protocol matter at least as much as backbone choice.

Key Findings

🏆
ConvNeXt-Tiny has the highest AUC point estimates in Exp 1, Exp 2, and Exp 4. ResNet-50 is a close second; the advantage is not statistically significant under paired tests at this single training seed.
📉
Celeb-DF → FF++ (Exp 3) collapses to near-chance (~0.51–0.59) for every backbone. Training-set scale and diversity dominate cross-dataset behaviour more than architecture choice.
🔄
ViT-Base does not lead under this leakage-controlled, modest-data regime. The convolutional inductive bias is advantageous over the attention mechanism when training data is limited.
⏱
Training-free temporal consistency fusion blends spatial mean score with frame-score variance at inference time — no retraining required: fused = 0.7 · spatial_mean + 0.3 · frame_std.
🌊
Noise is the most damaging corruption (AUC drops to ~0.49 under heavy noise), followed by blur, downscaling, and JPEG compression.
